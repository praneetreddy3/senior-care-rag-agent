# Fall Prevention and Home Safety

## The scope of the problem

Falls are a leading cause of injury among older adults and can lead to fractures,
loss of independence, and hospitalization. Many falls are preventable through a
combination of health management and environmental changes.

## Risk factors

Common fall risk factors include muscle weakness and balance problems, certain
medications (especially those causing drowsiness or dizziness), vision impairment,
low blood pressure on standing, foot problems and unsafe footwear, and home hazards
such as clutter, poor lighting, and lack of grab bars.

## Home safety checklist

- Remove tripping hazards such as loose rugs, cords, and clutter from walkways.
- Improve lighting, especially on stairs and along the path to the bathroom.
- Install grab bars near the toilet and in the shower or tub, and handrails on
  both sides of stairs.
- Use non-slip mats in the bathroom and keep frequently used items within easy
  reach.
- Ensure the person wears well-fitting, non-slip footwear.

## Health-based prevention

Beyond the home environment, fall prevention includes regular review of medications
for side effects that affect balance, vision checks, strength and balance exercises
appropriate to the individual, and management of conditions that affect blood
pressure and gait. A care team may recommend physical therapy to build strength and
balance.

## Responding after a fall

When a fall occurs, the care team should be notified promptly so the event can be
assessed, documented, and used to update the participant's care plan and reduce the
risk of future falls.
