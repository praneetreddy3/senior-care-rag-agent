# PACE Program Overview

## What PACE is

The Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) is a Medicare and Medicaid
program that helps older adults meet their health care needs in the community
instead of going to a nursing home or other care facility. PACE provides
comprehensive medical and social services to certain frail, community-dwelling
older adults, most of whom are dually eligible for both Medicare and Medicaid.

PACE is administered jointly by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS)
and individual state Medicaid programs. Organizations that offer PACE must be either
public entities or nonprofit private entities meeting federal and state requirements.

## The interdisciplinary team

A defining feature of PACE is the interdisciplinary team (IDT). Each participant's
care is planned and delivered by a team that typically includes a primary care
physician, a registered nurse, a social worker, physical and occupational therapists,
a dietitian, a recreational therapist or activity coordinator, home care staff,
personal care attendants, and a driver. The IDT is responsible for the initial
comprehensive assessment, periodic reassessments, and the ongoing care plan.

The IDT model is central to how PACE coordinates care: rather than a participant
navigating many disconnected providers, one team holds shared responsibility for
the person's medical, functional, and social needs.

## Services covered

PACE covers all Medicare- and Medicaid-covered care and services, and anything else
the interdisciplinary team decides is necessary to maintain or improve a
participant's health. Covered services commonly include primary and specialty
medical care, prescription drugs, nursing, adult day care, home care, physical and
occupational therapy, social services, meals, nutritional counseling, and
transportation to the PACE center and to medical appointments.

Because the program is fully capitated, the PACE organization receives a fixed
monthly payment per participant and assumes full financial risk for their care.
This aligns incentives toward preventive care and keeping participants healthy and
independent at home.

## The PACE center

Most PACE services are delivered at or coordinated through a PACE center, which
combines an adult day health center, a primary care clinic, and a hub for therapy
and social activities. Participants may attend the center for care, socialization,
and meals, and the same organization coordinates any care delivered at home or in
other settings.
