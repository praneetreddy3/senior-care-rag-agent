# Medicare and Medicaid Basics

## Medicare

Medicare is the federal health insurance program primarily for people aged 65 and
older, and for some younger people with disabilities. It has several parts:

- **Part A (Hospital Insurance)** covers inpatient hospital stays, care in a
  skilled nursing facility, hospice care, and some home health care.
- **Part B (Medical Insurance)** covers certain doctors' services, outpatient care,
  medical supplies, and preventive services.
- **Part C (Medicare Advantage)** is an alternative that bundles Part A and Part B
  (and usually Part D) through private plans approved by Medicare.
- **Part D** adds prescription drug coverage.

Most people qualify for premium-free Part A if they or a spouse paid Medicare taxes
for a sufficient number of quarters. Part B and Part D generally require monthly
premiums.

## Medicaid

Medicaid is a joint federal and state program that helps cover medical costs for
people with limited income and resources. Unlike Medicare, eligibility and covered
benefits vary by state. Medicaid often covers services Medicare does not, such as
long-term services and supports (LTSS), including nursing home care and many
home- and community-based services.

## Dual eligibility

People who qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid are called "dual eligibles."
For these individuals, Medicare is generally the primary payer and Medicaid may
help cover premiums, cost-sharing, and services not covered by Medicare. Dual
eligibles are the core population served by programs like PACE, which integrate
both funding streams into a single, coordinated benefit.

## Why integration matters

When Medicare and Medicaid operate separately, coverage gaps and administrative
friction can make care harder to coordinate. Integrated programs pool the funding
and place a single accountable organization in charge of the full range of a
person's care, which supports preventive, person-centered service delivery.
