# Medication Management and Safety

## Why it matters for older adults

Older adults often take multiple medications for several chronic conditions, a
situation known as polypharmacy. Polypharmacy raises the risk of drug interactions,
side effects, and confusion about how and when to take medicines. Careful
medication management is one of the highest-leverage activities for keeping older
adults safe at home.

## Medication reconciliation

Medication reconciliation is the process of creating the most accurate possible list
of all medications a person is taking — including name, dosage, frequency, and
route — and comparing that list against new orders at every transition of care.
Reconciliation is especially important at hospital admission and discharge, when
medication regimens frequently change.

## Common operational practices

- Maintain a single, current medication list accessible to the whole care team.
- Reconcile medications after every hospitalization, specialist visit, or change
  in condition.
- Watch for high-risk combinations and duplicate therapies.
- Support adherence with pillboxes, blister packs, reminders, and caregiver
  involvement where appropriate.
- Educate participants and caregivers on the purpose of each medication and the
  signs of adverse effects.

## Deprescribing

Deprescribing is the planned, supervised reduction or stopping of medications that
may no longer be beneficial or may be causing harm. It is always done under
clinical supervision, weighing benefits and risks for the individual.

## Role of decision-support tools

Tools that help staff quickly retrieve medication policies, formularies, and prior
notes can support safer workflows — but they must never replace clinical judgment.
A responsible assistant grounds its responses in source documents, cites them, and
clearly defers to a licensed clinician for any individual medical decision.
