# Caregiver Support and Respite

## The role of family caregivers

Family and informal caregivers provide a large share of the day-to-day support that
allows older adults to remain in their communities. Caregiving can be deeply
meaningful, but it is also demanding and can affect the caregiver's own physical and
emotional health. Supporting caregivers is therefore part of supporting the
participant.

## Common forms of support

- **Education and training** on the participant's conditions, safe transfers, and
  daily care tasks.
- **Respite care**, which gives caregivers a temporary break while the participant
  is cared for at a center, at home, or in a short-stay setting.
- **Adult day services**, which provide supervised care and activities during the
  day, allowing caregivers to work or rest.
- **Support groups and counseling** to reduce isolation and help caregivers manage
  stress.
- **Care coordination**, so caregivers have a single point of contact rather than
  juggling many providers.

## Signs of caregiver strain

Warning signs include exhaustion, sleep problems, withdrawal from activities,
irritability, anxiety or depression, and declining health. Care teams should check
in with caregivers regularly and connect them to resources before strain becomes a
crisis.

## Integrated programs and caregivers

Integrated programs such as PACE explicitly consider caregivers in the care plan.
By providing day services, transportation, home care, and respite, these programs
share the caregiving load and help sustain the informal support that keeps
participants at home.
