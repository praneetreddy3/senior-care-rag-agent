# Care Coordination Workflow

## Purpose

Care coordination is the deliberate organization of a participant's care activities
across the people and services involved, so that care is safe, timely, and aligned
with the participant's goals. In an integrated senior-care setting, the
interdisciplinary team (IDT) owns this process end to end.

## The coordination cycle

1. **Comprehensive assessment.** On enrollment, each discipline on the IDT assesses
   the participant across medical, functional, cognitive, nutritional, psychosocial,
   and home-safety dimensions.
2. **Care plan development.** The IDT synthesizes assessments into a single care
   plan with measurable goals, assigned owners, and target dates.
3. **Service delivery.** Services are scheduled and delivered at the care center,
   in the home, or through network providers, with transportation arranged as needed.
4. **Monitoring and communication.** The team meets regularly to review status,
   surface new problems, and update the plan. Documentation is shared so every team
   member sees the same current picture.
5. **Reassessment.** Care plans are reassessed on a defined schedule and whenever a
   significant change in condition occurs.

## Transitions of care

Transitions — such as a hospital admission or discharge — are high-risk moments for
older adults. Good coordination during transitions includes timely notification to
the IDT, medication reconciliation, a follow-up visit or call within a short window
after discharge, and clear communication with the participant and caregivers about
warning signs and next steps.

## Documentation and escalation

Reliable documentation is the backbone of coordination. Each interaction, change in
condition, and care-plan update should be recorded promptly. Teams typically define
escalation paths so that urgent changes (for example, a fall, a new infection, or a
sudden functional decline) trigger rapid review rather than waiting for the next
scheduled meeting.

## Role of tooling

Software that surfaces the right information at the right time — retrieving relevant
policies, prior notes, and care-plan details — can reduce the cognitive load on
coordinators and shorten the time it takes to answer routine operational questions.
Any such tool should ground its answers in source documents and make those sources
visible so staff can verify them.
